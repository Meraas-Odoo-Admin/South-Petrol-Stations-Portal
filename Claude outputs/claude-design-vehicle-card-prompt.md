**New piece for the Fuel Control System: the printed vehicle card.** A vehicle's owner receives this plastic card when the vehicle is registered at a police-station registration desk. At the petrol pump, the station employee scans its QR code with a smartphone camera to identify the vehicle, then enters the fill amount. Design it in the same visual language as the system you've already designed. It's the physical face of the same product, not a separate brand.

**Exact format — a hard constraint, not a suggestion:**
- Standard plastic ID-card size (ISO ID-1, also called "CR80"), used **vertically**: **53.98 mm wide × 85.60 mm tall**.
- At 300 dpi, that's **638 × 1011 px** per side.
- Corners rounded at **3.18 mm (about 38 px)**. Show the rounded corners on the artboard so the composition respects them.
- Keep every piece of text, the emblem and the QR at least **3 mm (about 35 px)** inside every edge. Card printers can shift the print by up to about a millimeter. Only background color or pattern may run to the edge.
- Design two artboards: **front** and **back**.

**Front, top to bottom, one centered column:**
1. Header: the issuing security authority's emblem (use a clearly marked placeholder — the official emblem needs permission first), the authority's name, and the card title "بطاقة تعبئة الوقود". Use "مديرية أمن سبها" as the sample, but the name changes with the municipality that issued the card, so leave room for a name up to about twice as long.
2. QR code: **30 × 30 mm (about 354 px)**, pure black on pure white, centered, with a plain white margin around it at least 4 QR squares wide. Nothing may touch or sit behind this area — no pattern, no tint, no overlapping graphics. Use a real, scannable sample QR that encodes the placeholder `SBH-7K2M9Q4X`.
3. Plate number: the largest text on the card, written left to right, e.g. `8-1234567`. It's the first thing the pump employee compares against the actual car.
4. Vehicle make, type and color on one line, e.g. "تويوتا هايلكس — أبيض".
5. Small line: card number `SBH-7K2M9Q4X` (left to right), issue date, issuing station code.

**Back:** short, plain Arabic instructions in the system's procedural voice (no exclamation marks):
- Show this card to the station employee to scan.
- This card belongs to this vehicle only and may not be transferred.
- If the card is lost or damaged, visit the nearest registration station.
- A contact number (placeholder).

**Constraints that come from how it will be printed:**
- **The final card is generated by the system as a PDF**, and your design is the reference it's built from. So keep to what reproduces exactly on a small plastic card: flat colors and solid shapes. No shadows, blurs, glows, photographic textures or mesh gradients — they print muddy on card printers and won't match your design.
- **The QR and all small per-card text are pure black, #000000.** Card printers use a separate, sharper black layer for pure black; near-black gets mixed from colors and prints soft.
- **Minimum text size is 6 pt**, and no line thinner than 0.25 pt. Anything smaller breaks up on a 300 dpi card printer.
- **Use one Arabic typeface** — the system's own, if it has an embeddable license. It will be embedded in the PDF.
- **Items 2–5 on the front change on every card.** Later, the fixed design will be pre-printed in color in bulk, and only these per-card items will be printed on top, in black only. So every per-card field must sit on a plain, light area and stay fully readable printed in black. Mark those per-card zones clearly on a separate annotation layer, e.g. with a thin outline and a small label.
- **Never show a person's name or national ID anywhere on the card.** It identifies the vehicle, not the owner, and it will live in a hot car in Sabha, where it can be lost.

**Use plainly fake sample data only** (the plate, card number and vehicle above), in real Arabic, with no placeholder English on the card itself.
